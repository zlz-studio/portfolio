03

VFX & Cinematics

Effects and cutscene systems built for shipped games. Each card plays its clips in order.

03.1

Cinematic cutscenes

Take animator-delivered character and camera animation into Timeline, then build everything around it: lightweight sets, per-shot VFX, volume grading and lens work, all synced in one sequence.

03.2

Impact frame

Full-screen shader and render feature for anime hit emphasis. Flash bursts, contrast breaks and chromatic pulses, timed to motion. No cost during regular gameplay.

03.3

Stencil buffer FX

Portal transitions and reveal masks built on the character shader and render features. Multi-layer masking that holds up under fast camera moves without sorting issues.

03.4

Character effect system

A character-specific effect system built on my character shader for the "Awakening" mode. It runs only while the mode is active, so normal gameplay pays nothing in FPS.

03.5

FX smoke

Anime-style smoke from a stylized texture, a shader and a custom mesh, with control over shape, direction and flow. No sprite sheets: a single 512×512 texture stays sharp and saves VRAM on mobile.

04

Optimization

Regulus: The Advent on a low-end Android phone. Same scenes, before and after my optimization pass.

Average FPS · 8 scenes 9.8 → 30.9
Speed-up 3.2×

How

01 · Asset budgets

  • Limits for 2D, 3D, rig and animation set from day one
  • Textures packed into RGB / mask channels
  • Mobile resolutions and ETC2 / ASTC formats
  • Material rules to keep draw calls low
  • One import-settings flow for every team

02 · Shader & rendering

  • Lightweight shaders, unused code stripped
  • Tight multi-compile keyword usage
  • SRP Batcher kept at full efficiency
  • Less overdraw, fewer transparency issues
  • Fewer passes; branch only when needed

03 · Diagnose

  • Frame Debuggerdraw calls, batching, sorting, stencil
  • Unity ProfilerCPU / GPU ms, spikes
  • Memory Profilertextures, meshes, peaks
  • RenderDocpixel cost, instruction count
  • Android Logcatstability, runtime errors
05

Case studies

Rendering problems default Unity doesn't handle for anime characters, and how ZLZ Anime Shader solves them. Drag the divider to compare.

05.1

Depth-safe transparent hair

Anime hair is usually opaque, so eyes and brows disappear behind it. Switching to transparent brings sorting bugs: the face renders in front, hair layers swap, back faces pop through.

A custom visibility pass keeps the hair transparent while depth stays correct. Eyes read through the fringe from every camera angle, with no sorting glitches in motion.

Default ZLZ
05.2

Silhouette-accurate hair shadow

Shadow maps give blurry, jittering hair shadows even at 4K, and the shapes never match the actual strands.

  • Sharp at any camera distance
  • Follows hair animation exactly
  • Independent of light direction
  • Stable 60 fps on mobile, no 4K cascades

05.3

Face shadow

A dedicated face shadow driven by the head direction system, so nose and cheek shading keep a clean, drawn shape as the head turns instead of the noisy terminator a standard lit shader gives.
